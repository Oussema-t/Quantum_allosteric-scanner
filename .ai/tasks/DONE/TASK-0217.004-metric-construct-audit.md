## The class

A metric that is correctly computed, statistically sound, and **measures
something other than what its name asserts**. Distinct from the other three
subtasks: nothing degrades, no criterion is unreachable, no anchor is wrong.
The arithmetic is right and the label is wrong.

**Confirmed instance — [[TASK-0208]] V3.** `backbone_explained` was defined as
the RMSD reduction achieved by transplanting the holo backbone with apo side
chains. But the two reconstructions are not symmetric: `holo_backbone +
apo_chi` moves N/CA/C **and mechanically carries every side chain with it**,
while `apo_backbone + holo_chi` moves only atoms beyond Cβ. The backbone
therefore collects credit for displacement the side chains would have
undergone regardless.

The consequence was a reversed verdict, not a noisy one. PTP1B's pocket
backbone rotates 21.3° while its side chains rotate 149.9° — a
side-chain-dominated change — yet `backbone_explained = 0.539` labelled it
"substantially backbone". A scale-free per-residue dihedral attribution put
the backbone share at **0.205**, and below 0.5 on all four targets.

The metric measured *how much displacement is carried by backbone motion*.
Its name asserted *how much of the change is backbone-driven*. Those diverge
whenever the backbone moves at all.

## Retrospective test

Rediscover the carriage confound in `backbone_explained` from its definition
alone, without being told the answer. If the method cannot, it will not find
the unknown instances.

## Intent Contract

- Outcome: for each reported metric in `RESULTS.md`, a short statement of what
  it actually measures, flagged where that diverges from what its name or
  surrounding prose asserts.
- In Scope — the questions to ask of each metric:
  1. **Decomposition symmetry.** If it attributes an effect between two
     components, can one component mechanically claim the other's share?
     (This is exactly what caught V3.)
  2. **Conjunctive criteria.** If it is an AND of two legs, which leg
     actually binds? [[TASK-0204]] D3 found the "druggability" hit criterion
     was in practice decided by its *overlap* leg — a structure-similarity
     proxy that any repacking destroys — while druggability stayed high.
  3. **Scale/normalisation.** Is it comparable across targets of different N?
  4. **Name–definition drift.** Does the prose around it claim more than the
     formula supports?
- Priority order for the audit (highest claim-bearing first): the proximity
  floor components, `overlap_frac`/`druggability_score`, `stratified_auc`'s
  well-powered-max, `side_chain_explained`/`backbone_explained`,
  `effective rank`, the frustration gap.
- Out Of Scope:
  - Redefining any metric. This produces statements and flags; redefinition
    is a separate, per-metric decision.
  - The statistical layer (nulls, LOD, multiplicity) — clean, leave it.
- Constraints:
  - Report the denominator: metrics audited vs. flagged.
  - Where a metric is fine, say so explicitly — a clean audit result is
    evidence and belongs in [[TASK-0184]] as much as a defect does.
- Planned Validation: the retrospective test; plus, for any newly-flagged
  metric, construct a case where name and measurement demonstrably diverge —
  as the dihedral attribution did for V3. A flag without such a case is a
  suspicion, and must be labelled one.

**#3 (stratified well-powered-max AUC) — clean, verified directly.**
Read `graph_walk_null` (`scripts/graph_walk_matched_null_rerun.py`): the
null distribution is built by computing the *identical*
`_well_powered_max(stratified_auc(...))` statistic on every one of the
20,000 permutation replicates, not a simpler proxy. The "pick the best
shell" selection this statistic performs on the real data is performed
identically on every null draw, so the look-elsewhere effect from
shell-selection is absorbed into the null comparison rather than leaking
into the reported p-value uncorrected. This is the same reasoning
question 2 flagged #1/#2 with, applied here and found to hold.

**#5 (effective rank) — clean, verified directly.** `correlation_matrix_
and_rank` (`scripts/observable_effective_rank.py`) uses a **Spearman**
(rank-based) correlation matrix, not raw covariance — scale/unit
differences across the 28 observables (question 3) cannot bias the
participation-ratio rank by construction. On name-definition drift
(question 4): "effective rank ~3" is a claim about the *static
correlational footprint* of the observable set, and the register's own
prior write-ups ([[TASK-0199]]/[[TASK-0207]]) already state this
distinction explicitly ("a mechanism's detectability under intervention
and its footprint in static correlational structure are... different
properties") rather than silently overclaiming it as causal
independence — the guard this question looks for is already present in
the prose, not missing.
