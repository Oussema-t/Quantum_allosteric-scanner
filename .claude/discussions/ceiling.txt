Let me reflect on each, sharpen where there's a subtlety, then weave them in.

**On "are we sure they're quantum" — the question splits, and the split is the answer.** A Hamiltonian isn't quantum or classical by itself; it's a Hermitian matrix. What's quantum is the *evolution*. So the question decomposes into two tests with very different answers:

The mechanical one — "is the coherent evolution correct" — is cleanly testable against analytics, and these are the small-graph tests you want. On an infinite line / large cycle with an adjacency Hamiltonian, the CTQW transition amplitude is a Bessel function: P(0→x, t) = J_x(2t)². On a dimer (N=2) it's Rabi oscillation, cos²/sin². On a cycle C_n the eigenvalues are exactly 2cos(2πk/n). And for any connected graph the Laplacian has exactly one zero eigenvalue with a constant eigenvector — multiplicity equals the number of connected components. That last one is not just a correctness test; it's a free data-cleaning detector, and your own output already trips it: the prody warning "More than 1 (2) zero eigenvalues" in cell #8 means some structure is feeding you a *disconnected* graph. So the nullspace test and the data-cleaning point are the same test wearing two hats.

The scientific one — "does coherence buy anything" — is the test that actually matters, and you already have the instrument: the Haken–Strobl ω knob. Sweep ω from 0 (fully coherent) to 1 (fully classical) and watch the AUC. If it's flat, coherence is irrelevant and your "quantum walk" is a graph kernel. Cell #30 already shows AUC_HS at 0.503 (ω≈0) vs 0.483 (ω large) for KRAS — flat. So your own data is whispering that the answer is "no, not on these targets," which is precisely what cell #37's verdict concluded independently. Formalize this as *the* quantum-relevance test and the honest headline writes itself: "coherence is correctly implemented and demonstrably does not contribute signal on cryptic allosteric pockets." That's a real result, not a failure.

**On propagation time — you've found a genuine hole.** The CTQW is time-averaged over `linspace(0.1, 20, 30)`, and on a *normalized* Laplacian t is dimensionless with no physical meaning; it's a free knob that moves the ranking. Two things to add: a sensitivity test (sweep t_max, measure top-5 Jaccard stability — if the ranking swings, the result is t-tuned, not physical), and a principled choice. The only parameter-free readout is the infinite-time average, P_∞(i,j) = Σ_k |⟨i|k⟩|²|⟨j|k⟩|² — but note that *is* the decoherent limit (it's the old "Mohtashim baseline" you had), so removing the free parameter also removes the coherence. That tension is worth stating outright: either you pick a spectrally-motivated finite t (e.g. ~1/spectral-gap, the mixing time) and own that it's a choice, or you take the infinite-time average and concede there's no coherence left to claim. Either is defensible; silently picking t=20 is not.

**On apo/holo superposition — this is the most important addition, because it can bound the ceiling physically before you fit anything.** Superpose holo onto apo on the aligned Cα (Kabsch/SVD), then do two things. First, map the holo pocket onto the apo frame *by 3D position*, independent of residue number — if the structure-mapped pocket and your sequence-alignment-mapped pocket disagree, you've caught a label bug geometrically. Cryptic allosteric pockets — and the KRAS Switch-II pocket is the textbook cryptic case — often do not exist in the apo conformation; they open only on binding. If the pocket region rearranges substantially apo→holo, then the apo contact graph physically does not encode the answer, and no topology-only method, quantum or classical, can recover it from apo. That single measurement may be the explanation for your near-chance numbers, and it converts "our method is weak" into "we quantified that cryptic pockets are absent from apo topology" — a finding, with a figure. So this isn't just data QA; it's a learnability gate per target.

Three cautions. Label it honestly as an optimistic upper bound and attach the block-bootstrap CI, because a 25k-trial max is an order statistic. Make it beat the right baselines — random residues, non-functional surface pockets (the challenge's own bar), *and* degree/betweenness centrality — because a ceiling of 0.6 that plain node degree also reaches is not your method's ceiling, it's just structure. And read what your own data already implies: optimized AUC_apo on KRAS was ~0.53. If a heavy search with the answer key in hand can only reach ~0.55, the ceiling is at the floor, and that — combined with the apo/holo RMSD finding — is very likely the true story: the signal isn't there to extract. Knowing that costs days and saves weeks.

**The interwoven ordering**, then, with your additions as the spine rather than appendices:

Phase 0 is now physics unit tests *and* data-cleaning gates together, because they're both "is the substrate valid" — the analytical small-graph tests (Bessel line, dimer Rabi, cycle spectrum) plus the nullspace-equals-components test that doubles as the disconnection detector, plus the cleaning (waters/ions/altlocs/insertion-codes, chain-break handling, correct allosteric-vs-orthosteric ligand per target, apo/holo same-construct verification, structure-quality quarantine for myosin).

Phase 3 is LOPO, *only on targets whose ceiling clears the baselines*, and its headline number becomes the ceiling-minus-LOPO gap — the quantified cost of not knowing the answer, which is a genuinely interesting scientific quantity.

Both ideas are worth building in, and one of them — the mode-projection physics — is better than you may realize. Quick reflection, then I'll put the whole thing into a plan file in the repo.

**PyMOL cleanup automates almost entirely**, and I'd actually script it *without* PyMOL — gemmi or Biotite give you deterministic, headless, version-controllable cleanup (strip waters/ions/cofactors/alt-locs, keep `polymer.protein` plus `polymer.nucleic` for the MYC/Max:DNA case, select chains by biological assembly). PyMOL headless (`pymol -cq`) is fine if you want parity with your colleague's procedure, but a GUI tool in a reproducible pipeline is a smell. One nuance matters: "add missing residues" has a trap. Missing *residues* (unresolved loops) in the apo are often exactly the disordered regions that become the cryptic pocket on binding — so modeling them in would be fabricating the answer you're trying to blind-predict. The right move is to work on the apo/holo *common resolved set* after alignment, flag the gaps, and only ever bridge them virtually (for graph connectivity) rather than inventing coordinates. Treat an unresolved apo pocket loop as a finding, not a defect to patch. And "keep largest chain" is a per-target switch, not a default — hemoglobin/ATCase allostery is inter-subunit, myosin is a complex, MYC is a heterodimer on DNA.

**The mode-projection idea is sound and it's a known, citable method.** Superpose holo onto apo, take Δr = holo − apo per Cα, compute the apo ANM modes, and project: the cumulative overlap tells you how few modes carry the conformational change (Tama–Sanejouand show it's usually 1–3 low modes). That directly answers your "which modes must be involved." Your energy/time chain is right in spirit with one correction your MD background will want: proteins in solvent are *overdamped*, so the physical timescale of a mode isn't its oscillation period 1/ω but a relaxation time ~ friction/(κλ_k). So the honest readout is a relaxation timescale and a stored *elastic* energy ½κλ_k a_k², not a kinetic bound — and you calibrate κ by matching ANM fluctuations to crystallographic B-factors. The payoff is large: the dominant-mode timescale is the principled value for the CTQW propagation time, which closes the "is t meaningful" hole exactly. And it gives a sharp operator-design check — if the apo→holo change lives in modes your NES filter damps, you've filtered away the very signal you need.

**ProteinLens cross-check: yes, and it's diagnostic, not just decorative.** It's a published, peer-reviewed predictor (Barahona/Yaliraki bond-to-bond propensity, classical current-flow on an atomistic energy-weighted graph, with a web server). If it cleanly hits the apo pockets and you don't, your problem is the *operator*, not absent signal — the ceiling is reachable, keep going. If it *also* misses the cryptic apo pockets, that corroborates "the signal isn't in apo topology" and puts your honest negative result in good company. Either outcome sharpens your story. Adopt its *readout* ideas (the distance-bias quantile correction especially, and edge/current-flow pathway extraction — the real version of your green tube), but keep the all-atom energy-weighted graph only as a clearly-labeled ablation arm, because it fights the challenge's topology-only assumption and wrecks your coarse-graining/qubit budget.
