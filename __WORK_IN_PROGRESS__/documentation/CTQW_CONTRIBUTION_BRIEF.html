What does the quantum walk contribute?

A complete accounting of the continuous-time quantum walk's measured contribution to allosteric pocket prediction — the method, the numbers, the numbers that cut the other way, and the pre-registered conditions under which we would change our conclusion.

01The decision number

One statistic answers the investment question. Everything after this section exists so it can be checked, attacked, or overturned.

Geometry and cavity shape each carry real, unique, statistically robust information. CTQW carries none that survives their presence. This is not a claim that CTQW measures nothing — section 06 shows it demonstrably does. It is a claim about what remains once two much cheaper quantities are already in the model.

02Dataset and provenance

What was scored, where it came from, and what was excluded.

The frozen set

22 apo/holo pairs curated blind and frozen before any score was computed (config/candidate_targets_task0243.yaml). Curation preceded scoring; the list was fixed first. 20 of 22 are usable. HIV_INTEGRASE_MUT871 and HIV_INTEGRASE_MUT916 (apo 1M9D) are excluded throughout: their active-site seed resolves empty on all four chains. This contradicted the curation task's own claim that zero targets fell back to a proxy, and is recorded as a real defect on our side, not silently dropped.

Seed provenance

Active-site seeds across the 22: uniprot = 18, ligand = 2, pdb_site = 0, none = 2. No target in the scored set uses a top-degree topological proxy. This matters because an earlier register-wide audit found 9 of 13 targets in a previous config were seeded at graph hubs rather than real active sites — that defect is fixed here, and it invalidated a prior positive result of ours (see section 06, item 3).

Labels

The pocket label is holo drug-contact residues mapped onto apo numbering by Needleman–Wunsch global alignment, contacted at heavy-atom distance (not Cα), with active-site residues and terminal residues excluded. Heavy-atom contact is load-bearing: a Cα-only approximation recovers only 9 of 21 pocket residues on KRAS_G12C.

03Method, in full

Enough detail to reproduce without reading our code. Section 09 gives the commands.

The three blocks

  • Geometry — three z-scored per-residue baselines: degree_centrality (count of Cα within 8 Å), euclid_from_seed_centroid (straight-line distance to the seed centroid), hop_from_seed (BFS shortest path on the 8 Å contact graph). Note our convention: hop_from_seed returns negated distance, so higher means closer.
  • fpocket — per-residue druggability score of the apo cavity a residue belongs to; 0 if it belongs to none. All-atom cavity detection, no seed, no dynamics.
  • CTQW — time_averaged_ctqw_converged on build_H_new, incoherent, the exact infinite-time limit rather than a finite-t_max approximation. This is our register's fixed gauge, pre-registered before any number in this brief was seen.

Fitting and scoring

Per target, on apo coordinates, with seed rows excluded from every evaluation. 5-fold stratified cross-validation, 20 repeats, OLS fitted on the training folds via lstsq, scored strictly out-of-fold. Shares are expressed as fractions of discrimination above chance: (AUC − 0.5) / 0.5.

Why in-sample fitting is not used, and why that matters to you

Our first version of this decomposition was an in-sample OLS fit — 4 parameters against 16–17 positives. We flagged it as an optimistic ceiling and predicted that cross-validation would lower the stacked AUC and raise the unexplained share. It did, substantially: the unexplained share moved from a median 51% to 67%, and CTQW's share fell from ~5% to ~1%. If you reproduce this with an in-sample fit you will get materially better-looking numbers for every block, including CTQW. That is the single easiest way to accidentally generate a favourable result here.

Two attributions, and which one decides

Blocks are correlated — CTQW is 32–49% linearly redundant with geometry — so a sequential fit assigns shared variance to whichever block enters first. We therefore report both:

  • Shapley share — exact, all 3! = 6 orderings brute-forced, no sampling approximation. This splits shared credit fairly among correlated predictors. It answers: "of the total, how much should be attributed to this block?"
  • Added-last marginal — full model minus the model without that block. It answers: "if I already have the other blocks, what does this one add?"

Both are correct answers to different questions. For an investment decision the added-last marginal is the relevant one, because it is the increment you would be buying. We report the Shapley share too, prominently, including where it favours CTQW.

Read the CTQW row carefully — it is the row most open to misreading

+11% Shapley and −0.1% added-last are both real and both ours. The gap between them is not an inconsistency; it is the measurement of CTQW's redundancy with geometry. Shapley credits CTQW for variance it holds jointly with geometry. Added-last credits it only for variance nothing else supplies. The joint part is large; the unique part is zero.

If you quote the +11% as evidence the walk works, we will point at the −0.1%. If we quoted only the −0.1% and hid the +11%, you would be right to object. Both are in this brief for that reason.

05Closing the "your model was invalid" objection

The strongest remaining defence of CTQW was that we never gave it a valid elastic network model. We tested that directly.

The objection has real force. The standard validity check for an elastic network model is whether predicted mean-square fluctuations correlate with crystallographic B-factors. We had never run it. R2 — real solvent accessibility replacing the burial proxy

degree_centrality is a poor burial proxy — it scores below 0.5 as a standalone predictor on 6 of 9 targets. Swapping in real Shrake–Rupley SASA is a genuine improvement: SASA beats degree against B-factor on 11 of 14 targets, moving three from FAIL to MARGINAL. Signs came out physically correct on every target (buried ⇒ low B, exposed ⇒ high B).

We then rebuilt H_new term-for-term with SASA replacing degree in its burial term, recomputed CTQW on all 20 targets, and re-ran the identical Shapley attribution:

06Every number in our own record that favours CTQW

These are real results we published. We are surfacing them ourselves, with what happened to each. If you find one we have not listed, that is a gap in this brief and we want to know.

Favourable number
CTQW's Shapley share is +11%, Wilcoxon p = 0.033 — significantly above zero.
What happened to it

True, and it stands. But Shapley averages over orderings including those where CTQW enters first, so it receives credit for variance it shares with geometry. Its unique contribution, measured added-last, is −0.1% at p = 0.50. The two numbers together are the finding: large joint contribution, zero unique contribution.

Favourable number
On the two-stage design, CTQW beat every control including fpocket's own druggability — Fisher p = 0.053, MRR 0.625 vs 0.329, better on 3 of 4 targets.
What happened to it

That was n = 4. We extended it. At n = 7 the same statistic weakened to p = 0.164; at n = 20 on the frozen set CTQW's MRR was 0.161 against the composite's 0.304 and fpocket's 0.344. The effect weakened monotonically as the sample grew — the signature of a small-sample fluctuation. This is the single strongest argument against either thread signing a joint protocol at a 5-target scale.

Favourable number
PTP1B's dcc_low reached AUC 1.000 at p = 0.00275, clearing a Bonferroni bar of 0.003125.
What happened to it

It did not survive our own seed audit. The seed was the top-5 highest-degree residues, not an active site — targets.yaml declared a descriptive string rather than a chemical component code, no ligand matched, and the code silently fell back to a topological proxy. Under the correct UniProt catalytic site (Cys215 + P-loop) the same measurement gives AUC 0.598 at p = 0.567, with 1 of 9 seed residues in common. We have added a RuntimeWarning so that fallback can never again be silent.

Favourable number
On HIV-1 RT, CTQW reached p = 0.0005 — surviving a ×28 Bonferroni correction.
What happened to it

That p-value comes from a scattered label-permutation null. The identical score vector, structure, seed and 2000 permutations under a spatially contiguous null gives p ≈ 0.20 — a 400× spread attributable to nothing but the null construction. The AUC also sits below a plain hop-distance baseline (0.7538 vs 0.7773). We consider this the cleanest single localisation of the disagreement between our threads: it is one line of null construction, not physics, code, or data.

Favourable number
CTQW is not merely a distance score — held at constant hop distance it still discriminates, AUC 0.6305, p = 0.027.
What happened to it

This one is true and we defend it. Only ~39% of CTQW's variance is a function of hop shell; within-shell it discriminates above chance on 8 of 9 targets. Anyone claiming CTQW is "just distance in disguise" is wrong, and we corrected our own looser phrasing on exactly this point. The limitation is different: within-shell, CTQW scores 0.6305 against the best geometric baseline's 0.6292, winning 4 of 9, p = 0.496. Its non-distance signal is real and is the same signal degree and euclid already deliver.

Favourable number
Across 14 Hamiltonians the pocket consensus is Friedman p = 1.6e−224, W = 0.466, and the modal pocket is the true drug pocket.
What happened to it

This is your thread's number, included because it is the strongest one on the table and we do not want it left unaddressed. It measures agreement between operators, not validity. Operators sharing a confound agree perfectly and are wrong together — we measured that degeneracy directly: ~28 observables in our register collapse to a participation-ratio effective rank of 2.6–4.1. The question consensus cannot answer is whether the operator ranks the true pocket better than fpocket's own score already does on the same candidate list. That comparison is in section 04, and the answer at n = 20 is no.

07What would change our mind

Pre-registered, so neither side can move the goalposts after seeing a result.

Any one of these, and we update

  1. CTQW's added-last marginal becomes significantly positive (Wilcoxon p < 0.05, positive on ≥ 14/20) on a frozen untuned set of ≥ 12 targets, with geometry and fpocket both in the model.
  2. The quantum and classical arms separate. Same Hamiltonian, classical diffusion twin computed alongside. Currently ρ = 0.79 median, AUC 0.5635 vs 0.4930, Wilcoxon p = 0.7775 — indistinguishable, and the classical arm ranks the true pocket better on 6 of 14. A real separation on any target would be the first evidence in our register of the operator doing something diffusion cannot.
  3. CTQW beats a hop-distance ranker on the same candidate list at adequate n. Currently they tie at mean rank 5.71 (Wilcoxon p = 0.594), and the true pocket beats a hop-matched candidate null on only 2 of 7 targets (Fisher p = 0.50).
  4. A valid-ENM subgroup effect. If CTQW's marginal is positive specifically on the targets that pass B-factor validity and negative on those that fail, that is a real, mechanistically sensible result. We have not tested this subgroup split and it is the most promising unexamined analysis we know of.

What will not change our mind, and why

  • A better p-value under a scattered permutation null. Spatial autocorrelation makes that null anti-conservative by a measured 4.8×–42× in our own tests. Any comparison must use a spatially contiguous null, or report both side by side.
  • An in-sample or same-target fit. See section 03. Leave-one-target-out or cross-validation, or the number does not travel.
  • A result on ≤ 5 targets. Our own n = 4 result reversed at n = 20. We would apply this rule against ourselves equally.
  • Consensus among operators. Section 06, final item.

08When investing in CTQW would make sense — and why not yet

The honest case for the operator, stated as strongly as we can make it.

The measurements above are conditional on a benchmark, and we think the benchmark is the bigger problem. Three findings, all ours, all pointing the same way:

What we measuredResult
Pockets in van der Waals contact with the active site (< 4.5 Å, heavy-atom)19/33 (58%)
Pockets at genuine long range (> 20 Å)0/33 (0%)
Targets meeting a 15 Å separation bar4/20
Targets already ≥ 80% open in apo (static retrieval, not cryptic discovery)9/20 (45%)

fpocket druggability scores median AUC 0.854 on the already-open targets versus 0.515 — near chance — on the genuinely cryptic remainder. The benchmark bundles two different tasks into every number, ours included.

Our distality filter, MIN_HOP ≥ 2, turns out to mean a median of 7.5 Å, minimum 2.09 Å. It was never physically justified; we have replaced it with reported distance and an explicit site category rather than a binary filter.

The conditions under which we would invest

Allostery is not one phenomenon. A drug binding a cryptic extension of the catalytic cleft, a drug binding a distinct pocket on the same domain, and a drug binding an oligomeric interface are three mechanisms with different modelling requirements. Our benchmark is 58% the first kind and 0% the third — and a through-protein transport operator is least likely to add anything on exactly the contact-adjacent cases that dominate it.

A quantum-walk approach is worth investment when it is tested on genuinely distal, genuinely cryptic sites — the regime where cavity geometry alone cannot work because there is no cavity in apo and no contact path to the active site. We have essentially none of those in the current benchmark. Building that target set is, in our view, the highest-value Phase 2 work available to either thread, and it is a prerequisite for the operator getting a fair test rather than an obstacle to it.

Five places your reproduction will legitimately diverge from ours

  1. Alternate locations. We parse with altloc="all". Dropping to the default silently removes heavy atoms and corrupts every distance statistic.
  2. The hop_from_seed sign convention. It returns negated BFS distance. We shipped a bug from this ourselves — a filter that removed every candidate on every target before we caught it.
  3. Heavy-atom attachment. A same-length guard is required for functional_indices (which contacts holo heavy atoms against apo coordinates) but must not be applied to holo_pocket_mask, which maps holo→apo by Needleman–Wunsch. We had this wrong; it degraded pockets to a Cα approximation and moved HIV-1 RT's pocket from 5 residues to 16.
  4. Randomness in trial loops. Our EvoEF2 wrapper accepts no seed, so repeated "trials" write byte-identical inputs. We caught a published 4/4 result that was one computation repeated four times; it reproduced as 3/4. Check hashes before treating repeats as samples.
  5. Stage-1 recall. In any two-stage design, fpocket fails to propose the true pocket at all on ~36% of targets — 14/22 usable here, 7/11 on an independent set. Denominators must be targets attempted, not targets where stage 1 succeeded.

10Known defects in our own work

Listed because you would find them, and because a brief that omitted them would deserve to be discounted.

  • Stale published numbers. A bug fix shifted every floor and AUC and our headline triplets were not refreshed for weeks. Two of three mandatory targets flipped from below-floor to above-floor once corrected — a correction in our own favour that we had been under-reporting.
  • An unreproducible headline. A "decisive 4/4" ceiling result reproduced as 3/4, with one trial below the comparison arm's maximum. Root cause in defect 4 above.
  • A stale hypothesis register. Eleven hypotheses were recorded as untested after tasks had in fact tested them, causing us to report tested work as untested. Now fully reconciled with zero open entries.
  • Two curation errors in the frozen set — the empty-seed HIV integrase pair, contradicting the curation task's own claim.
  • Four targets whose ENM still fails validity, on which we cannot interpret our own CTQW results in either direction.
  • Bin edges in section 08 are ours, stated openly and chosen from physical meaning. They are not a literature taxonomy and should not be cited as one.

Every number in this brief was recomputed from committed result JSON before publication, independently of the write-ups that produced it. Where our internal write-ups and the underlying data disagreed, the data is what appears here and the discrepancy is noted in place. Scripts, configs, raw outputs and the full task records are in the repository; we will hand over any of it on request.
